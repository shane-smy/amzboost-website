Amazon PPC managed against margin, not ACoS

ACoS tells you what an ad cost against the sale it made. It says nothing about whether that sale was worth making. We start from the contribution margin of each SKU, work out what it can afford to pay for a customer, and manage the ad account to that number.

The number most ad accounts are missing

Break-even ACoS, per SKU, after everything Amazon takes.

A product with a 34% contribution margin can afford a very different ad cost from one sitting at 11%, and the gap between them is where most Amazon ad budgets quietly go. Scaling the second product looks like growth in the ads dashboard and shows up as a smaller bank balance.

Before we change a bid we build the margin picture: selling price, referral fee, FBA fee, cost of goods, returns rate, storage. That gives a break-even ACoS for every SKU, and a target that sits deliberately below it. From then on the question for any campaign is not “is ACoS down?” but “is this SKU still earning what we need at this volume?”

Why PPC-only rarely fixes it

The ad account is usually the symptom.

When advertising costs jump without a change in strategy, the cause is often somewhere else in the account: a listing that lost its indexing after a content overwrite, a variation split that moved reviews off the main ASIN, a stock-out that reset organic rank so ads now carry sales they used to get free.

A PPC-only agency cannot see those causes and cannot fix them if it does. Because we run the whole account, the fix happens where the problem is — and the ad budget goes back to doing the job it is meant to do.

Common questions

Do you charge a percentage of ad spend?

No. A percentage of spend pays an agency to spend more of your money. We charge a fixed monthly retainer based on catalogue size, marketplaces and scope.

What ACoS should we be aiming for?

There is no universal answer, and an agency that gives you one without seeing your costs is guessing. The right target comes from your contribution margin per SKU: what the product earns after Amazon fees, cost of goods and returns. We calculate break-even ACoS per SKU first, then set targets against it.

Do you use bid automation software?

We use tooling for data and monitoring, but bidding decisions are made by a person who knows the catalogue. Rules-based automation is good at reacting and poor at knowing that a SKU is about to go out of stock or has just had a fee change.

Who owns the advertising account?

You do. We work inside your own Amazon Ads account with full visibility of campaigns, spend and performance. If the engagement ends, the account, its history and its structure stay with you.

Can you take PPC only, without the rest?

Sometimes. We will say during the review whether advertising alone can fix what we found. If the real problem is catalogue or listings, taking the PPC budget and leaving the cause in place would be charging you for the wrong work.
