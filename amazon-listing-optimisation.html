Indexed, ranked, converting

Three different problems. Most listing work only addresses the third.

Indexed means Amazon associates the listing with a search term at all. A listing can be beautifully written and invisible for its best keyword because the term sits nowhere indexable, or because a suppressed attribute has knocked it out of a browse node.

Ranked means where it sits among the listings that are indexed — driven by conversion history, sales velocity, price, reviews and relevance.

Converting is what the shopper does once they arrive: images, price, reviews, the first two bullets, the variation picker. Rewriting bullets improves the third while leaving the first two untouched — which is why so many listing refreshes produce nothing.

What listing work covers

Keyword research
Search volume and share of voice for the category, your Search Query Performance data, and the terms competing ASINs are indexed for.
Titles & bullets
Written to current category style guides and length limits, prioritising the terms that carry volume, readable to a shopper skimming on a phone.
Backend & attributes
Search terms, subject matter, and the structured attributes that decide browse-node placement and filter eligibility.
Indexing checks
Verification that each target term actually returns the ASIN, before and after changes, rather than assuming the upload worked.
Images & video
Main image compliance, the sequence that answers objections in order, and mobile-first legibility of infographics.
A+ content & store
Brand story, comparison charts that help shoppers pick the right variant, and a store that gives Sponsored Brands somewhere worth sending traffic.
Variation families
Rebuilding split or broken families so reviews and rank consolidate on the right parent — one of the highest-value fixes on most accounts.
Compliance
Claims, restricted terms and documentation that cause suppressions, checked before they take a listing down rather than after.

Changes are sequenced, not dumped

On a ranking ASIN, how you change it matters as much as what you change.

Rewriting every field on a listing that already ranks is a way to lose positions you cannot easily get back. We keep the terms the listing is already earning from, change one layer at a time, and verify indexing and rank between steps so we know which change did what.

Every listing we manage is then watched daily by Seller Control Hub, so a content overwrite by Amazon — or by a distributor with Brand Registry access — is caught and reverted quickly instead of silently costing you a month of rank.

Listing work is part of full account management, and is also available on its own for brands that only need the catalogue put right.

Common questions

How do you know which keywords a listing should target?

From search-volume and share-of-voice data for the category, your own search-term and Search Query Performance reports, and what the listings currently ranking are indexed for. Not from guessing which phrases sound right.

Will changing a listing lose its ranking?

It can, if changes are made carelessly or all at once on a ranking ASIN. We sequence changes, keep the terms a listing already ranks for, and check indexing after each change rather than rewriting everything in one go.

Do titles still need to be keyword-stuffed?

No, and category style guides increasingly forbid it. Amazon's title length and formatting rules now cut off or suppress overlong titles, and shoppers skim the first few words. A title has to index and read as a product, in that order.

Is A+ content worth it?

For conversion, usually yes. Brand story and comparison modules also help shoppers self-select the right variant, which reduces returns. A+ content is not indexed the way bullets and the backend are, so treat it as a conversion tool rather than a ranking one.

Can you fix a listing that keeps getting suppressed?

Usually. Repeat suppressions normally trace back to an attribute, a claim in the copy, or missing compliance documentation rather than to the thing the notification names. We find the cause and clear it through cases.
