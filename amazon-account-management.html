What we manage inside Seller Central

Not a checklist we work through once at onboarding. These are the things we hold week to week.

Catalogue & variations
Parent-child families, browse nodes, flat files, duplicate and orphan ASINs, merges and splits done without losing reviews or rank.
Buy Box & pricing
Buy Box share monitored daily, hijackers and unauthorised sellers challenged, pricing rules set against margin rather than a race to the bottom.
Stock & replenishment
Cover days by SKU and marketplace, restock limits, IPI, ageing stock and removal decisions, and the forecast that keeps a hero SKU from going dark.
Account health
Policy warnings, listing suppressions, safety and compliance documentation, ODR and late dispatch, plus the cases needed to clear them.
Fees & reimbursements
Dimension and weight checks after every fee change, FNSKU and category errors, lost and damaged inventory claims inside the filing window.
Advertising
Managed in the same engagement by our in-house specialist, so the ad account and the listings are never pulling in different directions.
Content & brand
A+ content, brand store, images and video, Brand Registry, and defending the detail page against overwrites.
Reporting
Monthly contribution statement by SKU and marketplace, reconciled to settlements, with a call to go through what it says.

What usually turns up in the first month

On accounts we review, these are the recurring finds — none of them visible from an advertising dashboard.

  • Hero listings not indexed for their highest-volume keyword, while PPC pays for the same term
  • Variation families split by a flat-file error, so reviews and rank sit on the wrong ASIN
  • Products in the wrong size tier after a fee change, overpaying FBA fees on every unit
  • Reimbursement claims never filed, inside a window that is about to close
  • Stranded and suppressed inventory nobody is alerted to
  • Advertising scaled on SKUs that lose money after fees and returns
  • Old A+ content and images contradicting the current listing copy

The account review tells you which of these apply to you, with what each one is costing. See how we calculate that.

How the account is actually run

  1. Daily

    Seller Control Hub checks every managed ASIN for Buy Box loss, suppression, content change, price movement and stock risk. Anything that breaks is on our list that morning, not at the next review.

  2. Weekly

    Advertising optimisation against target margin, search-term and placement work, listing and content changes, case follow-up, and a short written update on what changed and why.

  3. Monthly

    Contribution statement by SKU and marketplace, reconciled to settlement reports, with a call on what to push, what to fix and what to stop advertising.

  4. Quarterly

    Range review, pricing strategy, new marketplace decisions, stock planning for peak, and a reset of the next quarter's priorities.

Common questions

Do you need admin access to our Seller Central account?

We work as users on your own account with the permissions the work requires, and read-only access during the initial review. You stay the account owner throughout, and access can be withdrawn at any time. We never ask a brand to sell through an account we control.

Do you manage Vendor Central as well as Seller Central?

Our focus is Seller Central 3P. We work with hybrid brands running both, but a pure 1P Vendor account is not our strongest fit and we will say so rather than take it on.

How quickly do account problems get picked up?

Every ASIN we manage is checked daily by Seller Control Hub, our own monitoring software. Buy Box loss, suppressions, content overwrites, price changes and stock issues are flagged the morning they appear.

Is advertising included in account management?

Yes for most engagements. Advertising is managed by our in-house PPC specialist as part of the same scope, so bids and listings are not being changed by two teams who never speak.

Will you work alongside our existing team?

Often, yes. Some brands keep stock planning or customer service in house and hand us the marketplace side. We will agree who owns what in writing before we start.
