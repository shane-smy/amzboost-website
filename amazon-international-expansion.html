Amazon US and EU expansion for UK brands

A new marketplace is a new P&L, not a copy of the listings. Freight, duty, local fees, return rates and ad costs all differ, and a product that earns 27% in the UK can lose money in Germany. We sequence the launch properly and measure each market on its own contribution.

The order matters more than the speed

Most failed expansions are not failures of marketing. They are listings that went live before the compliance, or stock that shipped before the demand was understood.

  1. Market and margin modelling

    Category demand and competitor pricing in the destination market, against a landed-cost model including freight, duty, local fulfilment fees and expected return rates. Some products should not cross the border, and it is cheaper to find that out now.

  2. Compliance and registrations

    VAT and EPR registrations, responsible-person requirements, labelling, safety documentation and category approvals. Started first because they take the longest and will hold a launch up on their own.

  3. Listings built for the market

    Keyword research in the destination language — not a translation of the UK listing. Local measurements, local claims, local images where the main image rules differ, and a catalogue structure that matches the local category tree.

  4. Stock and fulfilment

    Deciding between local FBA, Pan-EU, remote fulfilment or a 3PL, with the first shipment sized for the forecast rather than for a container discount.

  5. Launch and measure

    Launch advertising with a defined rank-building window, then the first full contribution statement for that marketplace — and an honest decision about whether it continues.

Already in several markets?

The common problem is not launching. It is not knowing which market is carrying the others.

Brands selling across UK, US and a handful of European marketplaces often look at one consolidated figure. Inside it, one market is usually funding two that have never covered their own fulfilment and advertising costs — and a third may be sitting on ageing stock that is accruing storage fees faster than it sells.

We split the numbers by marketplace, with landed cost and local fees attached, and make the call on each one: scale it, fix it, or close it and bring the stock home. See profit reporting for how the figures are built.

Common questions

Should we launch in the US or Europe first?

It depends on the product, the margin after freight and duty, and the compliance burden. The US is one marketplace with one language and higher competition; Europe is several marketplaces with translation, EPR and VAT registrations, but often less crowded categories. We model both before recommending one.

Can we just use the Build International Listings tool?

It will copy listings and hold prices to a rule, which is useful. It will not do the keyword research for the destination market, fix category or compliance differences, or tell you that your main keyword translates into something nobody searches for.

How long does a new marketplace take to set up?

Listings and advertising can be ready in weeks. The constraint is usually registrations and compliance — VAT, EPR, product documentation and labelling — which run to months in some European markets and should be started first.

How do we know whether a new marketplace is working?

The monthly contribution statement separates each marketplace, including freight and duty in landed cost, so a new market that is not covering its own costs is visible from the first full month rather than at year end.

Do you handle the VAT and EPR registrations yourselves?

We manage the process and work alongside specialist tax and compliance providers for the filings themselves. You get one timeline rather than four suppliers to chase.
