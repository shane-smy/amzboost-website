A Toys & Games brand — sales up 23%, profit up 107%

TOYS & GAMES · 4 MARKETPLACES · 2026 YEAR TO DATE

Growth and profit are not the same thing

This brand's sales grew 23% year on year. Its net profit grew 107%. Those two numbers moving at such different rates is the whole argument for managing an Amazon account on contribution rather than on revenue — the same 23% could just as easily have arrived with profit flat, or down.

In figures: sales from $3.80m to $4.67m, net profit from $269,060 to $556,736, and margin from 7.1% to 11.9% across four marketplaces. On a business this size, nearly five points of margin is worth more than another year of sales growth at the old rate.

It held as volume rose

Margin gains often come from simply selling less of the cheap stuff. That is not what happened here: units were up 33% over the same period, ahead of sales growth. The quarters show the pattern holding rather than being a one-off — in Q3 net profit tripled year on year and margin went from 5.3% to 13.1%; Q4 to date has profit up 75% with units up 42%.

What changed

The advertising account was restructured and its targets reset against each SKU's contribution rather than its revenue. Campaigns previously judged on ACoS alone were reassessed on what they actually earned after fees, cost of goods and returns, and the budget moved accordingly.

The catalogue was worked rather than maintained. The range was extended, the top performers analysed to understand precisely why they sold, and that read applied to the stragglers instead of leaving them to drift. Competitive analysis fed pricing and positioning, and the calls on events, deals and promotions were made on whether they protected margin and added something real for the end customer — not on whether they moved units.

None of it was set and left alone. The account is monitored daily through our own software and adjusted continuously, which is why the gains compounded quarter on quarter rather than arriving as a single step change.

Hans Grill — No.1 Best Seller in the US

HOME & KITCHEN · AMAZON US · MANAGED SINCE 2021

Built on margin, not volume

We have managed Hans Grill's US account since 2021. The strategy has been margin-led from the start: pricing set to protect contribution rather than to win a race to the bottom, and held there through continuous competitor research on where rival products sit and how far they will discount to move.

Deals that buy rank, not just volume

Promotions are planned rather than reactive — which deals to run, when, and at what depth, so discounting buys visibility and rank that holds after the promotion ends. Alongside it, positioning work that keeps the product clearly placed against the alternatives a US shopper is comparing it with.

How we talk about numbers

Every figure we publish comes from a reconciled contribution statement and names the period it covers. We do not quote percentage uplifts without a baseline, and we do not count revenue growth bought with advertising as a result.

If a client prefers not to be named we describe them by category and size, and say so. If a number cannot be evidenced from the data, it does not go on this page.
