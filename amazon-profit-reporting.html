Amazon profit reporting, by SKU and by marketplace

Every month you get a contribution statement for your Amazon business, reconciled to your settlement reports. Not sales and ACoS — what each product earned after everything Amazon took.

Request an account review

Available as part of account management, or on its own as an independent read on your numbers.

Why agencies do not offer this

Because it is accounting, and most Amazon agencies came out of media buying.

Producing a real contribution statement means reconciling settlement reports to sales, allocating fees that arrive on a different cycle from the orders that caused them, handling reimbursements and returns that land months later, and holding accurate cost of goods per SKU. It is slow, unglamorous work and it does not fit in an advertising dashboard.

It is also the only way to answer the questions that matter: which products actually make money, which marketplace deserves the next tranche of stock, and whether the growth on the chart is reaching the bank.

AMZ Boost does this work for clients already, alongside running their accounts. It is the reason most of them came to us.

What the monthly pack contains

Contribution by SKU
Revenue down to contribution profit and margin for every SKU, with break-even ACoS calculated from it.
Marketplace split
UK, US and EU separated, so an expansion that is losing money cannot hide inside a group total.
Settlement reconciliation
Reported figures tied back to what Amazon actually paid out, with the differences explained rather than smoothed over.
Fee change tracking
Referral, FBA, storage and low-inventory fees tracked month to month, so a size-tier change is caught in weeks not quarters.
Returns & reimbursements
Return rate by SKU with cost attached, plus reimbursements recovered and claims still open.
Trend & prior year
Rolling twelve months and like-for-like comparison, so seasonality is not mistaken for performance.
Watch list
SKUs below target margin, SKUs where advertising exceeds break-even, and stock that is costing more to store than it will earn.
A call, not just a file
Half an hour on what the month says and what we are changing because of it.

What it changes

Decisions that are guesswork without it.

  • Setting advertising targets per SKU from break-even, instead of a blanket ACoS goal across the catalogue
  • Knowing which SKUs to delist, rather than discounting them into a bigger loss
  • Deciding whether Amazon US is earning its stock allocation or quietly subsidised by the UK
  • Pricing to protect margin when a fee change lands, in the same month it lands
  • Spotting a return-rate problem on a hero SKU before it reaches the reviews
  • Walking into a bank or investor conversation with Amazon numbers that reconcile

Common questions

Isn't this what Amazon's own reports show?

Amazon shows you sales, fees and ad spend in separate places, on different date bases, and does not know your cost of goods. Putting those together into a contribution figure per SKU — reconciled to what actually landed in the bank — is the work.

Do you replace our accountant?

No. This is management reporting for decisions, not statutory accounts. It sits between Amazon's raw data and your accountant, and most clients forward it to them because it makes the Amazon side of the year end far easier.

What do you need from us to produce it?

Cost of goods per SKU, including landed cost where you have it, and read access to Seller Central and Amazon Ads. We take the rest from settlement, fee and returns reports.

Can we get the reporting without full account management?

Yes. Some brands keep management in house and take the reporting on its own, as an independent read on what their Amazon business earns.

How far back can you rebuild?

As far as the reports go, subject to Amazon's data retention windows and having historic cost of goods. A twelve-month rebuild is usually enough to show the trend that matters.
